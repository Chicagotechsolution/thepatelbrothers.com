The food you get when a Patel family likes you.

We're two brothers from a Gujarati household where feeding people was the family sport. The Patel Brothers is that table, scaled up: a desi kitchen for everyday thalis and a catering side that sends proper trays — not sad hotel pans — to your weddings, pujas, office lunches, and Sunday somethings.

Our kitchen

Recipes with last names.

Every dish we'll serve has a source: Ba's kadhi, the dal our mother adjusted for thirty years and finally declared finished, the undhiyu our masi guards like a state secret and shared exactly once. We wrote it all down before opening a kitchen, because restaurants forget and families don't.

"Add more. They look hungry." — the only serving-size guidance we were ever given

The kitchen is vegetarian at its core, the Gujarati way, with a few Punjabi-leaning crowd-pleasers because this is America and someone always asks for paneer. Rotis are rolled to order. The jaggery is real. The chaas is free with every thali, and arguing about that was never an option.

The daily kitchen

Four thalis, rotating all week.

A thali is a complete answer to dinner: sabzi, dal or kadhi, rice, rotis, something crunchy, something sweet, chaas to wash it down. These four are our opening lineup; the sabzis inside them rotate with the market.

The house standard — how we actually eat.

  • Seasonal sabzi + ringan-style eggplant
  • Gujarati dal, sweet and tangy, or kadhi
  • Steamed rice, three rotis, papad

The Punjabi Guest $15

For the paneer people. We get it.

The Light One $12

Khichdi weather exists year-round.

  • Moong khichdi with ghee

The Sunday $17

Weekend-only. Portions ignore advice.
